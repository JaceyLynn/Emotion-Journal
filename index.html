        About this project

        Emotion Journal is a reflective journal for quickly documenting emotional reactions to events and discovering patterns across time.

        How AI participates

        1. You capturea moment in your own words
        2. AI interpretspossible emotion, context and trigger
        3. You correctevery interpretation remains editable
        4. AI comparespossible patterns emerge across your history
        5. You reflectpatterns are hypotheses, not conclusions

        The design question

        The question here is not how AI can tell someone what they are feeling. It is how AI can help someone notice patterns in their own experience while preserving their authority over interpretation.

        So the AI is framed as a hypothesis generator. It proposes; the person writing the journal decides. Its language is tentative on purpose: “I noticed…”, “This may be connected to…”, “One possible reading…”.

        Three roles for the AI

        Interpreter
        Reads one entry and proposes a possible emotion, trigger, intensity and a short observation.
        Pattern Finder
        Looks across entries the person has confirmed and proposes recurring relationships, such as an emotion that keeps appearing next to the same kind of situation.
        Reflection Layer
        Turns observations into a question. It never tells someone what they should feel, what diagnosis they may have or what to do.

        Human in the loop

        Every reading can be confirmed, partly accepted or rejected, and every label stays editable. When someone changes a reading, their version is what gets stored, and it is their version the Pattern Finder learns from. Patterns can be marked meaningful, maybe, or set aside.

        What is simulated here

        This portfolio demo simulates the AI interaction. The readings come from transparent, rule-based matching against a small vocabulary of emotions and triggers, and the sample journal is fictional. No text you write is sent anywhere: entries are kept in your browser’s local storage.

        Privacy, and the original prototype

        Personal journal content should remain private whenever possible. The original prototype explored local-model processing, running language models on the writer’s own machine through Ollama and LM Studio, so private journal content would not need to be sent to a third-party AI service. That version is kept in the repository’s prototype folder.
